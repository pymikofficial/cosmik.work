How I judge a workflow.

Five measures, and a log of what I shipped.

Measures

What it is, when it matters, how I move it. Show definitions

Human effort per task

What it is
The hours of people time a task needs from preparation through review, correction and rework. A workflow is only a win if this number falls and stays down.
When it matters
Any task repeated weekly or more often, where the manual baseline can be measured before the build starts.
How I move it
Map the task, time the manual baseline, remove steps first, and only then add automation to what is left.
In my work
A 30% cut in manual outreach at CampK12 across a 400+ person workforce. For the Toronto founder, 20 to 30 hours a month back, by his own estimate.

Output accuracy

What it is
The share of outputs that are correct and usable on real inputs without a person fixing them. Speed means nothing if quality slips.
When it matters
Before a workflow touches customers, money or data that people rely on.
How I move it
Test with real inputs and edge cases, add validation checks, and keep human review before any consequential action.
In my work
Python automation frameworks at Accenture for banking and healthcare clients cut post-deployment defects by 15%.

Turnaround time

What it is
The elapsed time from request to finished output, measured separately from effort. A fast workflow with a long queue is still slow.
When it matters
When people wait on the result, such as outreach, reporting, screening or onboarding.
How I move it
Run steps in parallel and remove handoffs that only pass work along.
In my work
Nine parallel B2B pipelines produced 2,314 contacts and $1.47M CAD of prospect pipeline in 90 days.

Adoption without help

What it is
The share of the intended users who run the workflow on their own without asking its builder for help. A workflow that depends on its creator is a liability.
When it matters
At handoff, and again after the first month of real use.
How I move it
Write a one page operating guide, train the team on their own tasks, and make the next step obvious at every point.
In my work
This is the handoff I aim for on every build. The SOP Generator on the Shipped page exists so the one page guide takes minutes, not an afternoon.

Cost per run

What it is
Model and tool spend per completed task, compared with the human effort it replaces.
When it matters
When volume grows and small per run costs start to add up.
How I move it
Use the smallest model that holds quality, cache repeated steps, and trim the input before it is sent.
In my work
The tools on the Shipped page cap input size, and the ones that handle people data strip contact details before any text reaches a model.

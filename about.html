About Soumik

Why it is called cosmik.work.

With the right guardrails, almost every business process can now run as an AI workflow. Lead research, onboarding, reporting, follow ups, the weekly status update nobody enjoys writing. When nearly all of the repeat work in a company can be rebuilt that way, the scale of the change is cosmic.

That is the idea behind the name. I am Soumik Chatterjee, and for 9+ years I have taken work that people repeat by hand and turned it into systems that keep running. Today I do it with AI, and cosmik.work is where that work lives.

The origin

Proof has always mattered more than claims.

I have kept things organised since before I could call it a skill. My paper trail starts in 1996, when I was six, and it has no gaps. Every certificate, mark sheet and piece of written feedback is kept and scanned. Not for nostalgia, but because a record you can check beats a story you have to trust.

It is the same reason every workflow I build starts with a measured baseline. If the gain cannot be shown in hours, accuracy or cost, it did not happen.

What I believe about AI workflows

Big idea, careful build.

01

Most work can be a workflow

If a task repeats and its decisions can be written down, it can run as an AI workflow. The hard part is writing the decisions down.

02

Guardrails come first

Validation checks, a log of every run, and a person approving anything that touches money, customers or data people rely on.

03

Measure before and after

Effort, accuracy, turnaround and cost, timed on the manual version first, so the gain is a number and not a feeling.

04

People own the exceptions

A one page guide and a named owner for the cases AI should not decide alone, so the workflow outlasts me.

The judgment

AI runs the repeat work. Reading people is still the job.

01

The complaint behind the complaint

A long-term US customer at CampK12 was unhappy, but the complaint did not add up. Spanish phrasing on her calls pointed at the real need, a bilingual coding instructor for her child, not a refund. I matched one and kept the year-long subscription.

02

The brief behind the job title

A recruitment client asked for a Senior Data Scientist on a budget that matched no such hire in the market. Instead of running a search set up to fail, I asked more questions. The real need was data visualisation with Tableau, Power BI and SQL. I reshaped the brief and filled the role.

03

A small habit that held a team

I joined CampK12 early in the pandemic, when loss and uncertainty were everywhere. I started a simple habit of thanking people by name in the team group. It stuck, and former teammates still bring it up years later.

04

Delivery over pride

Early in my career a piece of assigned work fell outside my skills. Rather than let the work stall, I sourced it through a freelance marketplace, checked it myself and delivered on time.
